# GSEB Class 1–12 Subject-Wise Question Paper Pattern Guide

This document compiles a practical Gujarat Board (GSEB/GSHSEB) reference from classes 1 to 12. Public secondary and higher secondary pattern references indicate that GSEB question papers commonly work on 100-mark structures with 3-hour duration in major board classes, and public summaries describe both SSC and HSC papers as divided into Part A and Part B or equivalent sectioned layouts with objective and descriptive components.[cite:33][cite:35][cite:36]

## Scope and use

The most clearly published pattern summaries available in public references are for SSC and HSC board classes, especially class 10 and class 12.[cite:31][cite:35][cite:36] For classes 1–8, and to some extent class 9 school-level exams, the tables below present Gujarat-board-style internal exam structures built from common subject groupings and language-paper conventions rather than claiming a single universally fixed state-board paper for every lower class.[cite:37][cite:43]

## Pattern assumptions for classes 1–8

GSEB board-level public blueprints are easiest to verify for SSC and HSC. For lower standards, schools typically conduct internal examinations, so the patterns below are framed as Gujarat-board-style school-paper structures using the subject families commonly taught in Gujarati, Hindi and English medium schools.[cite:37][cite:43]

## Detailed board-style pattern references for classes 10 and 12

### GSEB SSC pattern

Public SSC summaries describe class 10 question papers as 100 marks in 3 hours.[cite:33][cite:35] One widely cited pattern summary states that the paper has two parts, Part A and Part B carrying 50 marks each, with Part B subdivided into four sections testing knowledge, understanding, application and skills.[cite:33] Another public summary for language papers such as English, Gujarati and Hindi likewise describes 100 marks split into 50 descriptive and 50 objective marks.[cite:35]

### GSEB HSC pattern

Public HSC summaries describe most class 12 papers as 100 marks in 3 hours and note a division into Part A and Part B or equivalent objective/descriptive structure.[cite:36][cite:45] These sources also note that science-stream subjects such as Physics, Chemistry and Biology commonly carry 70 theory and 30 practical marks, while subjects like Mathematics and English may remain full-theory subjects.[cite:42]

## Stream-wise subjects in classes 11–12

### Science stream

A public school-level GSEB curriculum summary lists science stream subjects as English, Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics or Biology, Computer Applications, plus practicals in Biology, Physics and Chemistry.[cite:40] In actual papers, the recurring question-types include MCQ, very short answer, short answer, long answer, numericals, derivations, diagrams and practical/experiment-based prompts.[cite:36][cite:42]

### Commerce stream

A public school-level GSEB curriculum summary lists commerce stream subjects as English, Hindi or Gujarati, Accounts, Statistics, Economics, Organization of Commerce, and Computer.[cite:40] Public HSC passing-mark summaries also name Accountancy, Business Studies/Commerce, Economics, Statistics and Computer Application among 100-mark HSC subjects.[cite:42]

## Notes on accuracy

The strongest publicly visible evidence is for GSEB SSC and HSC paper patterns, especially class 10 and class 12.[cite:31][cite:33][cite:36] For classes 1–8 and some class 9 school exams, schools may vary the exact section names and mark splits, so those rows should be treated as a practical planning guide in Gujarat-board style rather than a claim of one mandatory official lower-class blueprint used everywhere.[cite:37][cite:43]
