# CBSE Class 1–12 Subject-Wise Question Paper Pattern Guide

This document compiles a practical CBSE-style reference for classes 1 to 12. CBSE publishes formal curriculum groupings for classes IX–X and XI–XII, including languages such as English, Hindi, and Gujarati, and core/elective subjects such as Mathematics, Science, Social Science, Accountancy, Business Studies, Economics, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics.[cite:3] For Class X English Language and Literature, the official sample paper for 2024–25 shows an 80-mark paper in 3 hours with three sections: Reading Skills, Grammar and Creative Writing Skills, and Literature Textbook.[cite:13]

## Scope and use

For classes 1–8, CBSE schools generally follow school-level periodic and annual paper formats rather than one national external board paper, so the patterns below are presented as CBSE-style school exam structures aligned to the subject groupings commonly followed in the curriculum.[cite:3][cite:21] For classes 9–12, the document gives more direct board-style structure because CBSE publicly specifies curriculum groups and sample question paper formats for secondary and senior secondary levels.[cite:3][cite:13]

## Pattern assumptions for classes 1–8

CBSE does not run a public national board examination for classes 1–8, so most schools use internally set papers with unit-test, term-end, and annual-exam formats. The detailed patterns below therefore show the question-types that typically appear in CBSE-style school exams rather than claiming one officially fixed board blueprint for every class in this range.[cite:3][cite:21]

| 11–12 | 80 | Reading, creative writing, literature | Reading comprehension, note-making/summaries in some patterns, invitation/reply, article/report, grammar/language conventions, extract-based literature, short and long critical responses.[cite:page:2][cite:9] |

## Detailed board-style pattern examples for classes 9–12

### CBSE English Class 10 official sample pattern

The official Class 10 English Language and Literature sample paper carries 80 marks in 3 hours and contains three sections: Section A Reading Skills for 20 marks, Section B Writing Skills and Grammar for 20 marks, and Section C Literature Textbook for 40 marks.[cite:page:1] Within this paper, the actual question-types include reading-comprehension MCQs and short responses, grammar correction/reporting/gap-filling items, formal letter writing, analytical paragraph writing, extract-based literature interpretation, 40–50 word answers, and 100–120 word extended responses.[cite:page:1]

### Typical CBSE secondary paper structure by subject family

| Subject family | Board-style sections | Typical question forms |
|---|---|---|
| Languages | Reading / grammar / writing / literature | Word meaning from passage, comprehension, grammar editing, paragraph, letter, extract-based questions, short and long answers.[cite:page:1][cite:6] |
| Mathematics | Objective to long-answer progression | MCQ, competency-based, short calculations, theorem/proof, case-based and long structured solutions.[cite:15][cite:page:2] |
| Science | Objective, short, long, competency-based | Assertion-reason, numericals, diagrams, experiment-based, explanation and long descriptive answers.[cite:15][cite:page:2] |
| Social Science | Objective, source/case, short, long, map | Source-based, assertion-reason, map work, analytical writing and theme-based responses.[cite:15][cite:page:2] |

## Senior secondary streams

### Science stream subjects (11–12)

CBSE senior secondary curriculum lists Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Mathematics, Applied Mathematics, Computer Science and related electives for the science-oriented course combinations.[cite:page:2] In these subjects, actual question papers usually combine MCQs, competency/case-based questions, numericals, derivations, diagrams, experiment-based prompts, and longer explanation questions, with practical components in several laboratory subjects.[cite:page:2][cite:9]

### Commerce stream subjects (11–12)

CBSE lists Accountancy, Business Studies and Economics among senior secondary electives.[cite:page:2] The actual question-types generally include objective items, short theoretical responses, case-study or application-based questions, journal/ledger/final accounts style numericals in Accountancy, and diagram or concept-application questions in Economics and Business Studies.[cite:9][cite:14]

## Notes on accuracy

CBSE officially standardises curriculum and publishes sample papers especially for board classes, while schools retain flexibility for lower classes.[cite:3][cite:13] Because of that, the most precise official structure is available for classes 9–12, and the class 1–8 rows should be used as a detailed school-exam planning guide rather than a claim of a single nationwide fixed paper blueprint.[cite:3][cite:21]
